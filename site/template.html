A craft skill for short films

Sizzle

Direct the film.
Measure every frame.

Sizzle helps an agent make a 15 to 90 second kinetic brand film, with a narrator, a score and cut-downs. It decides like a director before anything is generated, then checks the work on every frame and in the mix.

Bring an outline, a deck, a site, a brand template or a folder of photos. Sizzle finds the idea, writes the film on paper and passes a creative gate before it spends a credit on voice or music. You choose the text-to-speech provider.

Measuring the frames

    What changed

      Select a time to see that frame. A finding names the rule, the string and the measured number.

      Two jobs, in order

      Decide like a director.
      Prove it like a lab.

      Most generated films fail before the first frame: no insight, no turn, a script lifted from the deck, music chosen first. Sizzle makes those decisions on paper, then turns each quality bar into a measurement.

      Where the agent cannot see or hear, the audits and the user's review stand in.

      D

      The director

      An insight about the audience, a shape that acts out the client's promise, two ahas that each turn on one word, a motif whose states carry the story, lines written for the ear, and one craft move per scene.

      P

      The production

      A canvas page drawn as a pure function of time and rendered frame by frame. Every string and image is recorded as it is drawn, so size, reading time, collisions, motion, voice and loudness are measured rather than guessed.

      The order

      Paper first.
      Then sound and picture.

      Ten steps, always in this order. The film's state lives in files, so a new agent, or the same one after a long break, picks up exactly where the work stopped.

      No generation before the gate.

      Music, voice and sound effects spend credits and anchor the film.

      Nothing is generated until the idea, the script and the motif pass the creative gate and a person approves the storyboard. Once a score exists, the edit bends to it.

      1. Decide.

        Preflight the harness: eyes, voice provider, tools. Absorb the source, the brand and the audience. Find the insight, the proposition, the shape and the two ahas, then write the film on paper.

      2. Pass the gate.

        A fresh reviewer, or a separate pass, checks the package against fifteen points and names the weakest beat. Then a person approves a storyboard of the whole film, sketched frame by frame, before any credit is spent.

      3. Make, then prove.

        Generate the score, voice and sound; build the page; render and mix. Then run the layout and motion audits and the voice, loudness and speech checks, and write the numbers down.

      Decide; don't wait to be directed. A short ask is enough. Defaults cover length, voice, music, people and cut-downs, and each choice goes in the brief with its reason.

      Borrow the client's eyes and ears. The agent cannot listen and may not see, so the user gets the creative package at the gate and the score finalists before the edit.

      Before any credit is spent

      Fifteen points.
      One weakest beat.

      The creative gate reviews the film on paper. Every failure is fixed before the first generation call, and the reviewer names what to cut.

      Bring it to your agent

      One skill.
      A film you can defend.

      A film with a reason for every beat.

      Bring the audience, one proposition, source assets and a length. Start with a storyboard you can approve; production follows only after that approval and the creative gate.

      Rendering and provider calls require production tools, configured access and an approved budget.
