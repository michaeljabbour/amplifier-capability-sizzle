Film title: what the viewer will learn in one line

Purpose

What the product is for: the problem it solves, in plain words.

Who it is for: the audience, where they watch, what they already know.

Afterwards the viewer can: the one thing they will understand or be able to do.

Term one
Its question in plain words, then three everyday words that describe it.
Term two
Its question in plain words, then three everyday words that describe it.

How it teaches

One everyday example anyone can judge in a second. Two cases that fail in different ways, seen before they are named. One change at a time. A question, a silent pause, then the answer. A worked example, then the same idea on other things. A takeaway that stays on the last card. The product comes last.

The two ahas

Aha 1 at 0:19. Before: one sign is good and one is bad. After: each sits somewhere on a map, and where it sits says which way to move. Flip: the empty goal corner lights after two seconds of silence. Plant: the motif in the corner of frame 2, which becomes the goal corner.

Aha 2 at 0:44. Before: this is about signs. After: it is how to write anything people skim. Flip: the passing car becomes a thumb scrolling past an email subject line. Plant: the car that passes both signs without slowing.

Narration

  1. Line one, exactly as it will be spoken.
  2. Line two.
  3. Line three.

Questions for you

  1. Length: 60 s with a 15 s cut, or 45 s? Recommended: 60 s, so the pause can breathe.
  2. Is the example right for this audience? Alternative: a menu or an invitation.
  3. Voice and music: calm and warm, or brighter? Recommended: calm narrator, light piano and pizzicato.
